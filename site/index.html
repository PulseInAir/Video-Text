§ 01 — Capabilities

You've been retyping what's on screen

Pause. Squint. Type. Scrub back because you missed a word. Video Text replaces that loop with a rectangle.

01

Select like a screenshot

Drag a box over any part of the frame. Move it, resize from the corners, or take the whole video at once.

02

Auto-build transcripts

Leave Auto running and it reads each subtitle as it appears, skipping repeats. Export as a timed .srt.

03

Fix it before you keep it

OCR misreads things. Every capture stays editable — in the panel, and again later in your library.

04

Timestamps that hold up

Captures remember the second they came from, so links jump back and subtitle cues line up properly.

05

A library, not a clipboard

Everything is stored locally. Search it, filter by site, edit it, export any slice you want.

06

Nothing ever uploads

The OCR engine ships inside the extension. No servers, no keys, no requests. It works on a plane.

§ 02 — Method

Four steps, about ten seconds

Install it and it works on the next video you open. Nothing to configure.

01

Open a video

Any site with a standard HTML5 player — YouTube, TikTok, a lecture page.

02

Press Alt S

A selection box appears, already sitting over the subtitle strip.

03

Frame the text

Drag to move, pull a corner to resize. Tighter boxes read more accurately.

04

Grab it

Text lands in a panel you can edit, copy, save or export eight ways.

§ 04 — Compatibility
YouTubeFacebookInstagramTikTok X / TwitterVimeoTwitchReddit LinkedInCourseraUdemy Any HTML5 video

Netflix, Prime Video and Disney+ use DRM playback that returns blank pixels to every browser extension. Video Text reports that plainly rather than saving an empty result.

Your video never leaves your computer.

Most OCR tools upload your frames to a server and call it a feature. This one can't — the engine is bundled in the extension and runs in your browser. There is no backend to send anything to.

§ 06 — Questions

Reasonable questions

Q1Is it actually free?

Yes, and open source. No paid tier, no usage cap, no account. Recognition runs on your own machine, so there are no server bills to pass on.

Q2Does it work on Netflix or Prime Video?

No. DRM-protected players hand back blank pixels to every browser extension — a protection built into Chrome that nothing can work around. Video Text detects it and tells you, rather than quietly saving nothing.

Q3How accurate is it, honestly?

Strong on clean subtitles. Weaker on small, blurry or heavily compressed text — that's the nature of OCR, not something we're hiding.

If a result looks off: draw a tighter box, raise upscale to 3–4×, or switch layout mode to “single line”. Every capture stays editable, so fixing a stray character takes a second.

Q4Can it transcribe speech?

No — it reads text that is visually on screen, not audio. For videos with burned-in subtitles the result is much the same, which is what Auto mode is built for.

Q5Which languages?

English ships in the box. The underlying engine supports 100+ languages and more models can be bundled — open an issue if you need a specific one.

Q6Why does it run on every site?

Because a video can be on any page. The content script only draws the selection overlay when you press the shortcut or click the icon — it reads nothing from the page and sends nothing anywhere. The extension requests no broad host permissions at all.
